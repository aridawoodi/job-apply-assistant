How it works

Three steps, about thirty seconds to understand

It does the retyping. You keep the judgment calls and the Submit button.

  1. Once

    Import your resume

    Drop in a PDF or Word file. It builds your profile on your computer: contact details, work history, education and skills. You check it and answer a few things resumes don’t say, like work authorization.

  2. Each job

    Open an application, click Fill

    It fills what it knows from your profile, drafts the open questions with local AI, and asks you, one at a time, about anything it can’t answer.

  3. Always you

    Review the colours, then submit

    Every field is colour-coded so you see where each answer came from. You make the edits and you press Submit. It never does.

Fills the application for you

Name, contact details, links, work history, education, work authorization and your resume file. Live progress shows what’s required and what’s optional, and the panel asks you about anything it couldn’t fill.

Which keywords from the posting you already cover and which are missing, worked out on your computer. Then tailor your resume in one click.

Stages from Saved to Offer, notes, follow-up reminders and CSV export. It tracks when you press Submit, even if you filled the form yourself.

FAQ

Questions people ask first

Is it really free?

Yes. There is no paid plan, no credits, no trial and no in-app purchase. Every feature is free.

It can stay free because the AI runs on your own computer, so there is no server bill to pass on to you. The code is open source under the MIT license.

Does my resume leave my computer?

No. Your profile, resume, answers and application tracker are stored only in your browser on your computer. The AI runs on your device: Chrome’s built-in AI or the free Ollama app.

The extension has no account, no server, no analytics and no remote code. Equal-opportunity (EEO) answers and salary are never sent to the AI, not even the local one. Read the privacy policy.

Which job sites does it work on?

It fills applications best on Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby, with basic support for Workday and LinkedIn Easy Apply. On any other site you can copy any part of your profile with one click from the side panel.

A built-in practice application lets you try it safely before you apply for real.

Do I need Ollama?

Not necessarily. You need one local AI for the writing features, and the setup page picks for you. If your Chrome (138 or newer) supports its built-in AI, there is nothing to install; that usually needs about 16 GB of memory or a strong graphics card.

Otherwise it walks you through the free Ollama app, with no terminal. 8 GB of memory is enough, plus about 3 GB of disk space for the small model.

Does it submit applications for me?

No. It never presses Submit, Next or Apply. It fills the form, colour-codes every field so you can see what came from your profile and what the AI drafted, and you review and submit.

Will the AI make things up?

Tailored resumes and cover letters are fact-checked against your own resume. Invented employers, job titles, dates, numbers and tools are blocked, and you approve every change before anything is used.

Is it open source?

Yes, MIT licensed. The code is plain JavaScript with no build step, so you can read exactly what it does: github.com/aridawoodi/job-apply-assistant.

Is it the same as other extensions with similar names?

No. There are other Chrome extensions with similar names, and some of them sell paid features. This one is made by Ari Dawoodi, is 100% free, MIT open source and has no in-app purchases.
